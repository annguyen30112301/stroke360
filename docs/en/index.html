Why STROKE360 exists

An ordinary week on a neurology ward

Illustrative story, drawn from common situations faced by families of stroke patients.

  1. Monday, 6:40 am

    Lan's mother had a stroke while cooking breakfast. By noon she was on the neurology ward: paralysed on one side, unable to swallow, needing to be turned every 2 hours.

  2. Tuesday

    Lan took leave. Her brother lives in Binh Duong, her sister has a small child. Nobody knew how to feed Mom without her choking, and nobody dared to ask the doctor on rounds.

  3. Thursday

    Leave ran out. Lan hired a sitter through a domestic-help agency. The sitter was kind, but had never cared for a stroke patient, had been on duty for 3 days and nights at the next bed, and nobody supervised her. At work, Lan checked her phone every ten minutes.

Families don't lack love. They lack someone trained and supervised, and a way to know how their parent is doing today.

STROKE360 was built for exactly that first week, and for the months after the patient comes home.

We chose to do it differently

We don't compete to be the cheapest. We work to be trusted.

  • Caregivers are full employees with contracts and insurance

  • 5-day course advised by rehab doctors and written by nurses

  • 12-hour shifts per the Labour Code; night shifts by certified collaborators

  • A checklist each shift, and a diary sent to the family every evening at 18:30

  • A supervising nurse checks at the bedside at least twice a week

  • We stay with the family from hospital to home, through long-term care

Four values, and how we keep our word

Safety first

Target: zero serious incidents. We measure and report pressure sore, choking and fall rates to hospitals every quarter.

Transparent with families

Prices are public on the website. Referral commissions are disclosed. An evening diary for up to 5 relatives.

Respect for caregivers

Around 11 million ₫/month for about 228 hours, full insurance, and a path to supervisor.

Within our scope

No medication, no treatment procedures. Everything in hospital follows the orders of the ward's doctors and nurses.
